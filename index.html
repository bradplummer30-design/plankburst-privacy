Privacy Policy

PLANKBURST for iPhone and iPad · Effective date: 21 October 2026 · updated the same day for the release configuration with ads enabled, and on 22 October 2026 to name both ad networks in the section 4 heading and to add the Unity Ads disclosure detail (SDK user identifier, purchase history, ad reports), and later the same day to say how the ad-quality component's device-keyed data is treated in the App Store's App Privacy section

This policy describes how PLANKBURST ("the game", "we") handles information. PLANKBURST is made by Bradly Plummer, an independent developer. Questions and privacy requests: skyflip2026@gmail.com.

This page describes PLANKBURST version 1.0 for iOS as configured for release: interstitial ads are enabled (section 4) and Remove Ads is offered. Earlier test builds had the ad software switched off; the release candidate and the test builds in use from 21 October 2026 have it on, using the ad provider's test inventory during testing.

1. What the game stores on your device

PLANKBURST has no accounts and no sign-in. Everything the game itself keeps is saved only on your device, inside the game's own data container:

  • your best distance;
  • your settings (sound on/off, SFX volume, Reduce Effects, Battery Saver);
  • the runner look you have chosen;
  • a record of the purchases that Apple has verified for this device (which items you own and when that was last confirmed).

We do not upload this information anywhere and we operate no servers of our own. There is no cloud copy: deleting the game deletes these values from the device and they cannot be recovered. "Offloading" the app (an iOS feature) keeps its data, so they return if you reinstall. Purchases are different: they belong to your Apple Account and can be restored (section 3).

2. What the game does not do

  • No account, login, user name or profile.
  • No analytics, crash-reporting or measurement service of our own. The game is not linked to a Unity Cloud project, and Unity's engine analytics, performance reporting and crash reporting are disabled in its configuration.
  • No access to your location, camera, microphone, photos or contacts. The game never shows Apple's tracking-permission request and never reads your advertising identifier itself. What the software from Unity inside the game does with the identifiers it receives is described in sections 3 and 4.
  • No chat, user content, social features or external links other than this policy, the support page and your mail app.

3. Purchases (Apple and Unity In-App Purchasing)

PLANKBURST offers one-time purchases: twelve runner skins (US $1.99 each) and Remove Ads (US $4.99); the Original Runner is always free. Purchases are made through Apple's App Store with your Apple Account. We never see or store your payment details. Prices shown in the Shop come from Apple in your local currency.

When you buy or restore, Apple gives the game a signed transaction record (product identifier, transaction identifiers, purchase date, ownership type such as Family Sharing, and your App Store storefront). The game uses it only to unlock what you bought and keeps the local ownership record described in section 1. Restore Purchases (in the Shop and in Settings) asks Apple to resynchronise your purchases for the same Apple Account; it never charges you again and it does not restore local scores or settings.

Purchases are handled with Unity Technologies' In-App Purchasing software ("Unity IAP"). Unity IAP sends purchase events to Unity: the product, its price and currency, Apple's transaction identifiers and signed transaction information, the game's bundle identifier and version, device information (model, operating system, language and region, disk size, time since boot), the time the game was installed, and Apple's identifier for vendors (a device identifier). PLANKBURST uses no other Unity service (no Unity account, analytics or cloud project), so the game creates no Unity player or installation identifier for you; the purchase events can carry such Unity identifiers only in games that use those services. The device and transaction identifiers link the events to your device, so this is personal data even though the game has no accounts and never sends your name or email. Unity processes transaction and purchase data on our behalf to operate the purchasing service, and separately as an independent controller for its own financial reporting, legal compliance, security and fraud prevention, under the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy. Unity keeps order records until we or you ask for their deletion and its service logs for 90 days (Unity's published figures). Unity's own uses of this data are governed by the policy linked above; whether purchase events are also used for advertising measurement across apps is not stated in Unity's documentation and has not yet been confirmed with Unity. This section will be updated when that confirmation exists. No such event is sent until you start a purchase. Then, separately, starting a purchase, cancelling or failing it, completing it, and the later completion of a purchase that was paid but not yet delivered to the game (for example after Ask to Buy approval) each send an event.

4. Advertising (Unity LevelPlay with the ironSource Ads and Unity Ads networks) – enabled

PLANKBURST shows short full-screen interstitial ads through Unity LevelPlay, using the ironSource Ads and Unity Ads networks (all Unity Technologies). An ad can appear only after at least three runs have ended and at least 90 seconds of active play in the game, and only when the Run Over screen appears after such a run (while the game is stopped); after the ad closes you are back on the same Run Over screen, and Run Again and Menu never start an ad. Never during a run, while paused, on the menu, or during a purchase or restore. After an ad, the count starts again (three more ended runs and 90 more seconds of play). Time in the menu, paused or in the background does not count. An ad is not shown every time: one must be available, and none is shown once you own Remove Ads (from the next launch the ad software is not even started).

The ad software (LevelPlay with the ironSource Ads and Unity Ads network components) starts when the game starts (unless you own Remove Ads) and collects and sends to Unity/ironSource: a device identifier (Apple's identifier for vendors; the advertising identifier is not available to it because the game never asks for tracking permission), your IP address and the country derived from it, device and app information (model, operating system, language, screen size, connection type, app version), ad activity (ads requested, shown, clicked, closed), and performance and diagnostic data about the ad software. Unity's own disclosure for the Unity Ads network also lists an SDK-level user identifier and purchase history among the data that network may process (the game itself passes no purchase information to the ad software, but Unity receives the game's purchase events through Unity IAP, section 3), and a report you make about an ad through the ad's own controls goes to Unity. This is used to select and show non-personalised ads, to measure and pay for them, to prevent fraud and to operate the ad service. The game sets the ad software to non-personalised ads, to "do not sell or share" and to treat every player as a child (section 7). These settings govern personalisation and sale; they do not by themselves mean that no cross-app tracking happens inside the ad provider's systems. Unity's published guidance for this ad software lists the device identifier as data that may be used for tracking for third-party advertising, and Unity's own policy says that a child-directed signal reduces its ads data collection to an IP address, an installation-specific identifier for contextual ads and a developer user identifier. How this applies to the configuration used here has not yet been confirmed with Unity. The game's configuration also lists ad-network identifiers for Apple's SKAdNetwork, Apple's privacy-preserving ad-attribution system, which is used only when ads run and involves no identifier about you. Apple's rules require your permission through App Tracking Transparency before any such tracking starts, and no tracking may occur without it. Ads are enabled in this version with these settings. We have not yet received the ad provider's written confirmation of whether its processing counts as "tracking" under Apple's definition; if it is confirmed to be tracking, a future version will ask for your permission first or keep ads off, and this policy will be updated first. The ad provider's practices are described in the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy (ironSource is part of Unity); Unity decides how long it keeps that data. There are no ad-personalisation settings in the game because ads are set to non-personalised for everyone.

Current state: ads are enabled in version 1.0 as submitted. Before the game is on the App Store, test builds may receive the ad provider's test advertisements or no advertisements at all; live advertisements start when the game is released. If the way ads work changes in a future version, this policy will be updated before that version is released.

5. Apple privacy manifests

The game ships Apple privacy manifests for Unity, Unity IAP and the ad software. They declare that these components may read file timestamps, free disk space, system boot time and user defaults for their own functionality (never for tracking), and that the ad-quality component may collect a device identifier, performance data and diagnostic data which its manifest declares not linked to you and not used for tracking. Because that data is keyed to a device identifier, the App Store product page's "App Privacy" section treats it as linked to you; the rest of that section is filled in from the same facts, and its tracking answer rests on the vendors' declarations described in section 4.

6. Email you send us

If you email skyflip2026@gmail.com, we receive your email address and what you write, and we use them only to answer you and to handle any follow-up. We keep a support thread only as long as needed to answer you and handle any follow-up, and we delete it when you ask; we have not set a fixed routine retention period. Email is handled by Google (Gmail) under Google's terms.

7. Children

PLANKBURST is made for teenagers and adults: its marketing is aimed at players aged 13 and over, and it is not part of Apple's Kids category. The game asks for no personal information at all and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Because its cartoon characters may also appeal to younger players, the ad software is set to treat every player as a child and ads are non-personalised for everyone. If the audience position or the ad configuration changes, this section will be updated first.

8. Your choices and rights

  • Ads: buying Remove Ads stops interstitial ads at once, and from the next launch the ad software is not started at all. The game never asks for tracking permission; ads are set to non-personalised for everyone (section 4).
  • Local data: delete the game to delete it.
  • Purchases: managed in your Apple Account; refunds are Apple's (reportaproblem.apple.com).
  • Access and deletion requests (GDPR, UK GDPR, CCPA/CPRA and similar laws): email us. If you have never started a purchase, no Unity purchase data about you exists; ad-serving data exists for every device the ad software has started on (section 4), and our email thread with you. A cancelled or failed purchase attempt does leave a record at Unity. For purchase data that Unity processes on our behalf, we instruct Unity to act; Unity acts only on our instruction for that data and locates records by the identifiers the purchase software sent (the device's vendor identifier and Apple's transaction identifiers, which are not the order number on your receipt). We will tell you what we can and cannot do for your request. For data that Unity controls itself (its own financial, legal and fraud records, and ad-serving data from the ad software), you contact Unity directly through the rights section of the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy; we will point you there and can forward your request, but we cannot identify or delete those records ourselves. Apple's purchase history is managed in your Apple Account. We do not sell personal information, and the ad software is set to "do not sell or share".
