This policy describes how PLANKBURST ("the game", "we") handles information. PLANKBURST is made by Bradly Plummer, an independent developer. PLANKBURST is intended for players ages 13 and older (13+). Questions and privacy requests: plummerbro@gmail.com.

This page describes PLANKBURST version 1.0.3 for iOS. The free game is ad-supported: ads appear only around the Run Over screen – an optional ad you can watch to continue a run, and an interstitial ad when you leave Run Over after every third finished run (section 4). Remove Ads is a one-time purchase that turns ads off. PLANKBURST is intended for players ages 13 and older (13+) (section 7).
If you still have version 1.0.2 (until the 1.0.3 update reaches your device): ads in 1.0.2 are optional and permission-based - the game asks your age band and whether to allow ads, and the ad software starts only if you chose 18 or older, allowed ads and allowed tracking. 1. What the game stores on your device

PLANKBURST has no accounts and no sign-in. Everything the game itself keeps is saved only on your device, inside the game's own data container:

  • your best distances (the dock and the rooftop chapter);
  • your settings (sound on/off, SFX and music volume, haptics, Reduce Effects, Battery Saver);
  • if an earlier version (1.0 to 1.0.2) asked you: your age band and your ads choice, kept exactly as you left them (version 1.0.3 asks neither; how they are used now is in sections 4 and 7);
  • that you have seen the welcome notice of version 1.0.3, and when (section 4);
  • the runner you have chosen;
  • the coins you have collected, the crew members you unlocked with coins, the rocket boards and flying capes you hold and when you bought them, and which game tips you have already been shown;
  • the state of your last run when it ended (distance, coins, whether its CONTINUE was used), so that its Run Over screen can be shown again after the game was closed;
  • from version 1.0.2, if you use the leaderboard: the best distance still waiting to be sent to Game Center (section 3a);
  • a record of the purchases that Apple has verified for this device (which items you own and when that was last confirmed).

We do not upload this information anywhere and we operate no servers of our own. There is no cloud copy: deleting the game deletes these values from the device and they cannot be recovered. "Offloading" the app (an iOS feature) keeps its data, so they return if you reinstall. Purchases are different: they belong to your Apple Account and can be restored (section 3). Coins and everything unlocked with coins are saved progress on this device, not purchases: Restore Purchases does not bring them back.

2. What the game does not do

  • No account, login, user name or profile.
  • No analytics, crash-reporting or measurement service of our own. The game is not linked to a Unity Cloud project, and Unity's engine analytics, performance reporting and crash reporting are disabled in its configuration.
  • No access to your location, camera, microphone, photos or contacts, and no notification permission request. The game never reads your advertising identifier itself; it shows Apple's tracking-permission request only in the situations described in section 4. What the software from Unity inside the game does with the identifiers it receives is described in sections 3 and 4.
  • No chat and no user content. The only social feature is the optional Game Center leaderboard, new in version 1.0.2 (section 3a). No external links other than this policy and the support page.

3. Purchases (Apple and Unity In-App Purchasing)

PLANKBURST offers one-time purchases: Remove Ads and the twelve Wild Crew runners. The Original Runner is always free. The twelve crew members are unlocked with coins you collect while playing, not with money; anyone who bought one of them in an earlier version keeps it, and Restore Purchases brings it back. Rocket boards and flying capes are bought with coins too. Remove Ads removes ads and includes one CONTINUE per eligible run. Purchases are made through Apple's App Store with your Apple Account. We never see or store your payment details. Prices shown in the Crew Shop come from Apple in your local currency.

When you buy or restore, Apple gives the game a signed transaction record (product identifier, transaction identifiers, purchase date, ownership type such as Family Sharing, and your App Store storefront). The game uses it only to unlock what you bought and keeps the local ownership record described in section 1. Restore Purchases (on the Remove Ads card in the Crew Shop) asks Apple to resynchronise your purchases for the same Apple Account; it never charges you again and it does not restore local scores, coins or settings.

Purchases are handled with Unity Technologies' In-App Purchasing software ("Unity IAP"). Unity IAP sends purchase events to Unity: the product, its price and currency, Apple's transaction identifiers and signed transaction information, the game's bundle identifier and version, device information (model, operating system, language and region, disk size, time since boot), the time the game was installed, and Apple's identifier for vendors (a device identifier). PLANKBURST uses no other Unity service (no Unity account, analytics or cloud project), so the game creates no Unity player or installation identifier for you; the purchase events can carry such Unity identifiers only in games that use those services. The device and transaction identifiers link the events to your device, so this is personal data even though the game has no accounts and never sends your name or email. Unity processes transaction and purchase data on our behalf to operate the purchasing service, and separately as an independent controller for its own financial reporting, legal compliance, security and fraud prevention, under the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy. Unity says it keeps transaction data for as long as it is needed to run the purchasing service, the transaction identifier as a hash so that a purchase is not processed twice, and its service logs for 30 days (Unity's published figures, November 2026). Unity's own uses of this data are governed by the policy linked above. Unity states that these purchase events are sent whatever your ad choices are, because the purchasing service needs them, and that an advertising identifier is added to them only when a game passes an advertising consent to Unity's own consent system; PLANKBURST does not do that, so none is added. Unity's published privacy declaration for this software describes its data as not used for tracking. No such event is sent until you start a purchase. Then, separately, starting a purchase, cancelling or failing it, completing it, and the later completion of a purchase that was paid but not yet delivered to the game (for example after Ask to Buy approval) each send an event.

Purchases and the ad software. If the ad software is running when a purchase is made, completed or restored – which is possible only when it was started under the conditions in section 4 – Unity's purchasing software also hands the transaction (the product, Apple's transaction identifiers and the signed transaction information) to the Unity Ads software inside the game. Unity's published App Store privacy disclosure for the Unity Ads software (it describes Unity's data practices as of August 2021 and is not written for one version of that software) lists purchase history among the data it collects, linked to the user and used for third-party advertising and analytics; that disclosure names the device identifier, not purchase history, as used for tracking. Unity's support team told us on 23 November 2026 that purchase history should be treated as collected whenever the Unity Ads software is running, whatever your answer to Apple's tracking question and whatever consent signals the game sends. So, if you make, complete or restore a purchase while the ad software is running – including after you chose “Ask App Not to Track” – we treat that purchase as collected by Unity and linked to you, for third-party advertising and analytics. This concerns only purchases you actually make or restore; if you buy nothing, there is no purchase to hand over. It is separate from the purchase events Unity's purchasing software sends to run the purchasing service (described above). Unity’s privacy policy governs how Unity Ads handles the transaction. Section 4 explains what Unity told us about how it and its advertising partners handle PLANKBURST purchase data when you choose “Ask App Not to Track.” If the ad software is not running – you owned Remove Ads when the game started, you had not yet answered Apple's tracking dialog, an earlier version recorded you as under 18, you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, or the App Store had not yet confirmed your purchases at that launch – nothing is handed over. If you buy Remove Ads while the ad software is running, the game stops requesting ads at once, but the software stays loaded until the app closes, so that purchase can still be handed to it as described above.

3a. Game Center leaderboard (optional; new in version 1.0.2)

From version 1.0.2, PLANKBURST has one all-time distance leaderboard in Apple's Game Center, Best Dock Distance. It ranks runs on the dock only; the rooftop chapter is not ranked. Version 1.0.1 has no leaderboard and sends nothing to Game Center. In version 1.0.2, nothing is sent to Game Center until you open LEADERBOARD in the game and are signed in to Game Center on your device. From then on the game sends the distance of eligible runs (the distance reached before a run's first fall) to Apple and shows the ranking Apple returns: ranks, distances and the Game Center names Apple provides. Game Center is Apple's service, under Apple's privacy policy; we receive no account details and keep no copy of the ranking. You control Game Center in iOS Settings > Game Center.

4. Advertising (Unity Ads through Unity LevelPlay) – the free game is ad-supported

PLANKBURST is free and ad-supported. It can show two kinds of short full-screen ads from the Unity Ads network, requested through Unity LevelPlay, Unity's ad mediation software (both Unity Technologies). In version 1.0.3 Unity Ads is the only network asked for ads; the ironSource Exchange that earlier versions also used is not used for 1.0.3's ads. There is no banner. Watch an ad to continue (optional): after the first fall of a run, when an ad is ready, the Run Over card offers WATCH AD TO CONTINUE above RUN AGAIN and MENU. Nothing starts unless you tap it. If you watch the ad to the end, the same run comes back once at a safe spot with your distance and coins kept; closing it early, an ad that fails, or closing the app during it gives no continue. Interstitial: the game counts finished runs in both chapters during one session; after every third finished run, when you tap RUN AGAIN or MENU, one short ad can appear over the Run Over card if one is ready, and the game then goes where you tapped. If no ad is ready you go there at once. No interstitial follows a run in which you watched an ad to continue. Never during a run, while paused, on the menu, or during a purchase or restore. No ad of either kind is shown once you own Remove Ads (the ad software is not started for an owner); owners get one CONTINUE per eligible run without an ad.

The first launch. When you first open version 1.0.3 – on a new install, or the first time after updating from version 1.0.2 – the game shows a short welcome notice: PLANKBURST is ad-supported, you may see an ad on the Run Over screen, Remove Ads is available in the Crew Shop, with a link to this policy. CONTINUE only closes the notice. It is not a consent to anything: the game records only that the notice was seen. The game does not ask your age.

When the ad software can run. The ad software (LevelPlay – its native part is the ironSource SDK, which also contains the ironSource network that 1.0.3 does not use for ads – with the Unity Ads component; the ad-quality module is not included) is started only when all of the following hold:

  • you do not own Remove Ads, and Apple has confirmed your purchases at this launch – until then nothing starts and you can play;
  • no earlier version of the game recorded you as under 18 – an answer of “under 13” or “13 to 17” saved by version 1.0 to 1.0.2 is kept and keeps ads off (section 7);
  • your device's region and your App Store storefront are both known and outside the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland – in those regions showing ads needs your prior consent, which this version does not ask, so no ad is shown and the ad software is not started there;
  • iOS has an answer to Apple's App Tracking Transparency question for PLANKBURST – either answer: “Allow” or “Ask App Not to Track” (or tracking is restricted on your device). Until there is an answer, nothing starts.

Apple's dialog is shown at most once per installation (iOS keeps your answer), after the welcome notice, and only to a player for whom the first three conditions hold and for whom iOS has no answer yet; if you answered it for an earlier version, that answer is used and you are not asked again. If you choose “Ask App Not to Track”, or tracking is restricted on your device, iOS does not give the ad software your device's advertising identifier (IDFA), and ads can still be shown: they are chosen without that identifier (contextual ads), with the same opt-out signals as for every player (below). The ad software still receives the other data listed below, including Apple's identifier for vendors and your IP address, and the app still declares Unity's ad servers as tracking domains (iOS decides what it allows). Unity's published documentation does not say, for players who did not allow tracking, that this data is not used to link you across other companies' apps or for ad measurement; Unity's documentation for its ad exchange says that when no advertising identifier is available, Apple's identifier for vendors is passed to the buyers bidding for the ad, together with your tracking status. Unity’s reply about purchase data did not address how ad buyers use the vendor identifier or other device data. Your answer changes nothing else in the game.

The privacy signals the game sends. Before the ad software starts, the game tells it, for every player, that there is no consent to personalised advertising and that the player opts out of the sale or sharing of personal information (do-not-sell). The game never tells the ad software that you are an adult. It marks requests as not child-directed, because PLANKBURST is a general-audience game intended for ages 13 and older and the game has no information that you are a child (section 7). Tapping CONTINUE, allowing tracking in Apple's dialog and the game's App Store age rating are never treated as consent to personalised ads, as permission to sell or share your data, or as a sign that you are an adult. If you chose NO ADS in version 1.0.2: that free switch no longer exists, so you see ads like other players, and the game sends you the same opt-out signals as everyone; nothing is charged and nothing is unlocked. An ALLOW ADS answer from version 1.0.2 is no longer used as consent.

What the ad software collects when it runs, and sends to Unity (Unity Ads and LevelPlay): your device's advertising identifier (IDFA – only if you allowed tracking) and Apple's identifier for vendors, your IP address and the country derived from it, device and app information (model, operating system, language, screen size, connection type, app version), ad activity (ads requested, shown, clicked, closed) and performance data about the ad software. Unity's own disclosure for the Unity Ads network also lists an SDK-level user identifier and purchase history among the data that network may process (section 3), and a report you make about an ad through the ad's own controls (the hand icon in the ad, then Report ad) goes to Unity. The networks use this data to serve and measure ads, limit how often you see them, pay for ads, prevent fraud and operate the ad service; Unity's own disclosures (for Unity Ads and for LevelPlay) describe the device identifier as used for third-party advertising and tracking, which is why the game asks Apple's question. The signals above ask them not to use your data for personalised ads and not to sell or share it; how each network applies those signals is governed by its own policy (Unity has told us that each network handles them under its own policies). The ad provider's practices and retention are described in the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy (ironSource is part of Unity). The game's configuration also lists ad-network identifiers for Apple's SKAdNetwork, Apple's privacy-preserving ad-attribution system, which involves no identifier about you.

Changing your mind. Tracking permission is Apple's: change it in iOS Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking (or Settings > PLANKBURST). If you turn it off, iOS stops giving the advertising identifier to the ad software (iOS normally restarts the app when this setting changes); ads can still be shown without it, under the same opt-out signals. Turning tracking off does not stop ads: Remove Ads does, on every device that restores it.

Purchase data when you choose “Ask App Not to Track.” On iOS, if you choose “Ask App Not to Track,” we configure our ad setup (Unity LevelPlay with Unity Ads as the only ad network, advertising consent set to false, and the do-not-sell option enabled) so that Unity and its advertising partners should not link purchase data from this game with data from other companies’ apps or websites for ad targeting or measurement. Ads in this situation are limited to contextual advertising.

7. Audience: ages 13 and older, teens and children

PLANKBURST is intended for players ages 13 and older (13+). From version 1.0.3 its App Store age rating is 13+. It is not part of Apple's Kids category and is not directed to children under 13. The game does not ask your age and does not verify it, and it never uses its App Store age rating to treat you as an adult or as having agreed to tracking.

  • Players whose age is unknown – every player of version 1.0.3 unless an earlier version saved an age answer – are not treated as adults. Apple's tracking dialog can be shown to them (section 4). Once Apple's question is answered, the ad software may serve ads with the opt-out signals described in section 4 (no consent to personalised ads, do-not-sell) and is never told that the player is an adult; if tracking is not allowed, the ads are chosen without the advertising identifier.
  • Teens. A player whom version 1.0 to 1.0.2 recorded as 13 to 17 (or under 13) keeps that answer: the ad software is never started for them, Apple's tracking dialog is not shown by the game, and no ad data about them is collected. A teen without such a saved answer cannot be told apart by the game and is handled like any player whose age is unknown (above), with the same opt-out signals.
  • Children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has used the game and you want data about them removed, email us (section 8).

If the audience or the ad configuration changes, this section will be updated before the version that changes it is released.

8. Your choices and rights

  • Ads. You can change PLANKBURST’s tracking permission in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. In version 1.0.3, eligible players who choose “Ask App Not to Track” can still see contextual ads. The ad software does not receive Apple’s advertising identifier (IDFA) in that case. For either tracking choice, PLANKBURST requests non-personalized ads and tells the ad service that you opt out of sale or sharing. Remove Ads turns ads off on each device where the purchase is verified or restored. If you still use version 1.0.2, section 12 explains your ad controls.
  • Local data: delete the game to delete it.
  • Purchases: managed in your Apple Account; refunds are Apple's (reportaproblem.apple.com).
  • Access and deletion requests (GDPR, UK GDPR, CCPA/CPRA and similar laws): email us. If you have never started a purchase, no Unity purchase data about you exists; ad-serving data exists for every device the ad software has started on (section 4), and our email thread with you. A cancelled or failed purchase attempt does leave a record at Unity. For purchase data that Unity processes on our behalf, we instruct Unity to act; Unity acts only on our instruction for that data and locates records by the identifiers the purchase software sent (the device's vendor identifier and Apple's transaction identifiers, which are not the order number on your receipt). Unity offers no self-service tool for such requests: we pass yours to Unity's purchasing support with the details you can give us (the product, the date, your Apple receipt), and we tell you what Unity could and could not locate. For data that Unity controls itself (its own financial, legal and fraud records, and ad-serving data from the ad software), you contact Unity directly through the rights section of the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy; we will point you there and can forward your request, but we cannot identify or delete those records ourselves. Apple's purchase history is managed in your Apple Account. We do not sell personal information, and for every player the game tells the ad networks that sale or sharing of personal information is opted out (section 4).

12. Version 1.0.2 (for players who have not updated yet)

Until version 1.0.3 reaches your device, ads in your version work as follows (the text of section 4 as published for version 1.0.2 on 18 November 2026):

PLANKBURST can show short full-screen interstitial ads through Unity LevelPlay, using Unity Ads and the ironSource Exchange (all Unity Technologies). There is no banner and no rewarded ad. Ads are optional: no ad is shown and the ad software is not started unless you allow it (below); software started in a session stays loaded until the app closes. When ads are allowed, an ad can appear only after at least three runs have ended, and only when the Run Over screen appears after such a run (while the game is stopped); after the ad closes you are back on the same Run Over screen, and Run Again and Menu never start an ad. Never during a run, while paused, on the menu, or during a purchase or restore. After an ad, the count starts again (three more ended runs). Time in the menu, paused or in the background does not count. An ad is not shown every time: one must be available, and none is shown once you own Remove Ads (the ad software is not started for an owner).

When the ad software can run. On the first launch the game asks for your age band (under 13, 13 to 17, 18 or older) and, if you chose 18 or older, whether to allow ads. If you allow ads, the game then asks iOS for App Tracking Transparency permission (Apple's own dialog, shown once per installation). The ad software (LevelPlay with the ironSource and Unity Ads components; the ad-quality module is not included) is started only when all of the following hold: you do not own Remove Ads (your purchases are checked with Apple at each launch before anything starts), you chose 18 or older, you allowed ads, and iOS tracking permission is allowed. In every other case – while a choice is still open, after you decline, after you withdraw, and when tracking is denied, restricted or not yet answered – the ad software is not started at that launch and no ad is shown. The age band is a neutral choice kept on your device; the game asks no birth date and does not verify it.

What the ad software collects once you have allowed ads and tracking, and sends to Unity/ironSource: your device's advertising identifier (IDFA) and Apple's identifier for vendors, your IP address and the country derived from it, device and app information (model, operating system, language, screen size, connection type, app version), ad activity (ads requested, shown, clicked, closed) and performance data about the ad software. Unity's own disclosure for the Unity Ads network also lists an SDK-level user identifier and purchase history among the data that network may process (section 3 describes how purchase transactions reach the Unity Ads software while it is running), and a report you make about an ad through the ad's own controls goes to Unity. The networks use this data to choose, personalise and measure ads, which includes linking it with data from other companies' apps and sharing it with their advertising partners and attribution companies – that is “tracking” in Apple's sense, which is why iOS asks for your permission first – and to pay for ads, prevent fraud and operate the ad service. The game tells the ad software that you consented to advertising, that you have not opted out of sale or sharing, and that you are not a child; it never sends those signals for anyone under 18, anyone who has not chosen, or anyone who declined. The ad provider's practices and retention are described in the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy (ironSource is part of Unity). The game's configuration also lists ad-network identifiers for Apple's SKAdNetwork, Apple's privacy-preserving ad-attribution system, which involves no identifier about you.

Withdrawing. Settings > PRIVACY & ADS lets you change the age band, switch ADS off or on, and reach Apple's tracking setting. Switching ads off tells the ad software that your consent is withdrawn and that you opt out of sale or sharing, discards the ad it holds, requests nothing further and does not start the ad software at later launches; an ad already on screen finishes normally. Ad software that has already started cannot be stopped inside the running session (its maker offers no such control); it stops when the app is closed; until then, a purchase made in that same session is still handed to it as described in section 3. Turning tracking off for PLANKBURST in iOS Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking has the same effect as soon as the game returns to the foreground, and at every later launch. We have asked Unity to confirm details of its data handling (most recently in November 2026) and will update this policy when it answers; the game asks for your permission before any of the ad software can start.

Current state: ads are optional and permission-based in version 1.0.2, exactly as in versions 1.0 and 1.0.1. If the way ads work changes in a future version, this policy will be updated before that version is released.
